** 1. The Basics
Graphs, digraphs, multigraphs, bipartite graphs, trees, forests, connectivity, paths and cycles, Euler tours.

- Graph model: =:graph=, =:multigraph=, =:multidigraph=, =:digraph=, =:weighted-graph=, =:tree=

- Trees & Forests: =count-spanning-trees= (Matrix-Tree), =weighted-graph-mst=.

*** Example

A triangle has a cycle, is 3-colourable, and contains an Euler tour because every vertex has even degree.

#+BEGIN_SRC janet :session diestel :results none
(import graphs :prefix "")
#+END_SRC

#+BEGIN_SRC janet :session diestel :results output
(def tri (make-graph))
(add-edges tri [["a" "b"] ["b" "c"] ["c" "a"]])

(print "cycle?: " (has-cycle? tri))
(print "vertex degrees: " (string/join (map |(string (degree tri $)) (nodes tri)) " "))
(print "Euler tour: " (string/join (eulerian-tour tri) " "))
(print "node count: " (node-count tri))
(print "edge count: " (edge-count tri))
#+END_SRC

#+RESULTS:
: cycle?: true
: vertex degrees: 2 2 2
: Euler tour: a b c
: node count: 3
: edge count: 3

** 2. Matching, Covering and Packing

Bipartite matching, Hall's theorem, augmenting paths, the Gallai–Edmonds decomposition, factor-critical graphs, and the packing/covering duality (König, Dilworth, Menger).

- Bipartite matching by augmenting paths (Kuhn): =maximum-bipartite-matching=
- Hall condition test: =satisfies-hall-condition?=
- Matching predicates: =is-matching?=, =is-maximal-matching?=, =is-perfect-matching?=, greedy =maximal-matching=
- Bipartite: =bipartite-sets=, =bipartite-perfect-matching?=
- Packing/covering duality (König): =minimum-vertex-cover= and =maximum-independent-set= on a bipartite graph
- Packing: clique enumeration =find-cliques= and =find-all-cliques= (Bron–Kerbosch) packing via cover/independence duality.
- General (non-bipartite) maximum matching via Edmonds's blossom algorithm: =maximum-matching=, =is-perfect-matching-graph?=

*Unimplemented*: Gallai–Edmonds decomposition and factor-critical structure.

*** Example

König's duality on a small bipartite graph. The max matching and the min vertex cover coincide in size.

#+BEGIN_SRC janet :session diestel :results output
(def bp (make-graph))
(add-edges bp [["a" "1"] ["a" "2"] ["b" "2"] ["c" "3"]])

(def sides (bipartite-sets bp))
(def m (maximum-bipartite-matching bp (sides :x) (sides :y)))

(printf "bipartite sides: %s | %s"
        (string/join (sort (array/slice (sides :x))) ",")
        (string/join (sort (array/slice (sides :y))) ","))

(printf "max matching: %p pairs" (length m))

(each p (sort (array/slice m) (fn [a b] (< (string (a 0)) (string (b 0)))))
  (printf "  %s--%s" (string (p 0)) (string (p 1))))

(print "perfect? " (bipartite-perfect-matching? bp (sides :x) (sides :y)))
#+END_SRC

#+RESULTS:
: bipartite sides: a,b,c | 1,2,3
: max matching: 3 pairs
:   a--1
:   b--2
:   c--3
: perfect? true

The min vertex cover equals the max matching and the max independent set equals =|V| - ν=

#+BEGIN_SRC janet :session diestel :results output
(def k22 (make-graph))
(add-edges k22 [["a" "x"] ["a" "y"] ["b" "x"] ["b" "y"]])

(def sides (bipartite-sets k22))

(def m (maximum-bipartite-matching k22 (sides :x) (sides :y)))

(def cover (minimum-vertex-cover k22 (sides :x) (sides :y)))

(def indep (maximum-independent-set k22 (sides :x) (sides :y)))

(print "matching size: " (length m))
(print "vertex cover: " (string/join (sort (array/slice cover)) ","))
(print "independent set: " (string/join (sort (array/slice indep)) ","))
(print "|V|-nu: " (- (node-count k22) (length m)))
#+END_SRC

#+RESULTS:
: matching size: 2
: vertex cover: a,b
: independent set: x,y
: |V|-nu: 2

*** Example

Edmonds blossom on a non-bipartite graph.

The Petersen graph has a perfect matching, with two disjoint triangles, each an odd cycle, hence a genuine blossom.

(add-edges petersen [["0" "1"] ["0" "2"] ["0" "5"]

                     ["3" "5"] ["3" "8"] ["4" "6"] ["4" "9"]
                     ["5" "7"] ["6" "9"] ["7" "8"] ["8" "9"]])

(printf "Petersen max matching: %p pairs" (length (maximum-matching petersen)))
(printf "Petersen perfect? %p" (is-perfect-matching-graph? petersen))
#+END_SRC

#+RESULTS:
: Petersen max matching: 5 pairs
: Petersen perfect? true

** 4. Planar graphs

Euler's formula, Kuratowski/Wagner characterization, planar embeddings, planar embedding construction, dual graphs, and face enumeration.

- =is-planar?= checks planarity via Demoucron–Malgrange–Pertuisot (DMP)

*Unimplemented*: planar embedding construction, dual graphs, and face enumeration

*** Example

The two Kuratowski obstructions are the smallest non-planar graphs. They fail planarity tests for different reasons; $K_5$ is rejected with Euler's bound $m \leq 3n-6$, but $K_{3,3}$ satisfies the bound ($9 \leq 12$) and requires the face-splitting test. The $3 \times 3$ grid, with 12 edges and 9 vertices, is planar.

#+BEGIN_SRC janet :session diestel :results output
(def k5 (make-graph))
(add-edges k5 [[1 2] [1 3] [1 4] [1 5]
               [2 3] [2 4] [2 5]
               [3 4] [3 5]
               [4 5]])

(def k33 (make-graph))
(add-edges k33 [["a1" "b1"] ["a1" "b2"] ["a1" "b3"]
                ["a2" "b1"] ["a2" "b2"] ["a2" "b3"]
                ["a3" "b1"] ["a3" "b2"] ["a3" "b3"]])

(def grid33 (make-graph))
(add-nodes grid33 (range 9))
(each i (range 9)
  (when (not= 2 (% i 3)) (add-edge grid33 i (+ i 1)))
  (when (< i 6) (add-edge grid33 i (+ i 3))))

(printf "    K5: n=%d, m=%d, 3n-6=%d, planar is %p"
        5 (edge-count k5) (- (* 3 5) 6) (is-planar? k5))

(printf "  K3,3: n=%d, m=%d, 3n-6=%d, planar is %p"
        6 (edge-count k33) (- (* 3 6) 6) (is-planar? k33))

(printf "grid33: n=%d, m=%d, 3n-6=%d, planar is %p"
        (node-count grid33) (edge-count grid33)
        (- (* 3 9) 6) (is-planar? grid33))
#+END_SRC

#+RESULTS:
:     K5: n=5, m=10, 3n-6=9, planar is false
:   K3,3: n=6, m=9, 3n-6=12, planar is false
: grid33: n=9, m=12, 3n-6=21, planar is true

*** Example

Turán's theorem says $\text{ex}(n, K_{r+1})$ is equal to the edge count of the complete equitable $r$-partite graph $T(n,r)$, which =turan-number= and =make-turan-graph= compute independently. Agreement across $n$ and $r$ can be checked rather than assumed.

#+BEGIN_SRC janet :session diestel :results output
(printf "%3s %3s %14s %12s" "n" "r" "ex(n,K_r+1)" "|E(T(n,r))|")

(each [n r] [[6 2] [7 2] [9 2] [10 3] [12 4]]
  (printf "%3d %3d %11d %11d" n r
          (turan-number n r)
          (edge-count (make-turan-graph n r))))

(printf "\nT(6,2) chi=%d (K3-free, bipartite)"
        (chromatic-number (make-turan-graph 6 2)))

(printf "T(6,3) chi=%d (K4-free, 3-partite)"
        (chromatic-number (make-turan-graph 6 3)))
#+END_SRC

#+RESULTS:
:   n   r    ex(n,K_r+1)  |E(T(n,r))|
:   6   2           9           9
:   7   2          12          12
:   9   2          20          20
:  10   3          33          33
:  12   4          54          54
: T(6,2) chi=2 (K3-free, bipartite)
: T(6,3) chi=3 (K4-free, 3-partite)

** 10. Hamilton cycles

Hamilton cycle, Hamilton path, Hamiltonian, sufficient conditions. Theorems: Dirac, Ore, Pósa.

- =hamiltonian-cycle= and =hamiltonian-path= give exact backtracking search on general undirected graphs
- =hamiltonian-path-dag= for directed acyclic graphs
- =hamiltonian-possible?= predicate for necessary conditions (per-vertex degree and connectivity), and =hamiltonian-dirac-sufficient?= to check Dirac's sufficient condition (min degree ≥ n/2)
- =tournament-hamiltonian-path= since every tournament has a Hamilton(ian) path

*** Example

Dirac's condition (minimum degree at least $n/2$) is sufficient but not necessary.

$W_5$ has minimum degree 3 on 6 vertices, which satisfies the condition and is Hamiltonian, while $C_5$ has minimum degree 2, fails it, and is also Hamiltonian. The bowtie (two triangles meeting at a single cut vertex) passes =hamiltonian-possible?= since the vertices, connectivity and minimum degree conditions all hold, yet has no Hamilton cycle.

#+BEGIN_SRC janet :session diestel :results output
(def c5 (make-graph))
(add-edges c5 [[1 2] [2 3] [3 4] [4 5] [5 1]])

(def bowtie (make-graph))
(add-edges bowtie [[1 2] [2 3] [3 1] [3 4] [4 5] [5 3]])

# wheel W5: a 5-cycle plus a hub joined to all five
(def w5 (make-graph))
(add-edges w5 [["h" 1] ["h" 2] ["h" 3] ["h" 4] ["h" 5]
              [1 2] [2 3] [3 4] [4 5] [5 1]])

(printf "is W5 dirac-sufficient? %p (min degree %d >= n/2 = %.1f)"
        (hamiltonian-dirac-sufficient? w5)
        (min ;(map |(degree w5 $) (nodes w5)))
        (/ (node-count w5) 2.0))

(printf "is C5 dirac-sufficient? %p (fails, yet C5 is Hamiltonian)"
        (hamiltonian-dirac-sufficient? c5))

(printf "C5 cycle: %p" (hamiltonian-cycle c5))

(printf "bowtie degrees: %p"
        (sort (map |(degree bowtie $) (nodes bowtie))))

(printf "bowtie possible: %p (all necessary conditions hold)"
        (hamiltonian-possible? bowtie))

(printf "bowtie cycle: %p (exact search finds none)"
        (hamiltonian-cycle bowtie))

(printf "bowtie path: %p (a Hamilton path still exists)"
        (hamiltonian-path bowtie))
#+END_SRC

#+RESULTS:
: is W5 dirac-sufficient? true (min degree 3 >= n/2 = 3.0)
: is C5 dirac-sufficient? false (fails, yet C5 is Hamiltonian)
: C5 cycle: @[1 2 3 4 5]
: bowtie degrees: @[2 2 2 2 4]
: bowtie possible: true (all necessary conditions hold)
: bowtie cycle: nil (exact search finds none)
: bowtie path: @[1 2 3 4 5] (a Hamilton path still exists)

** 11. Random graphs

- =random-graph=, The G(n,p) model
- =random-graph-edges= G(n,m) plus =barabasi-albert= preferential attachment
- All three accept an optional seed for reproducibility

*** Example

Two features of =random-graph=, one asymptotic and one exact.

For $p=c/n$ the number of isolated vertices in $G(n,p)$ converges to a Poisson distribution of mean $e^(-c)$ so their proportion tends to $e^(-c) > 0$. For a single small sample the edge count is checked against its binomial expectation $p·C(n,2)$.

#+BEGIN_SRC janet :session diestel :results output
(def g (random-graph 12 0.4 7))

(print "nodes: " (node-count g))
(print "edges: " (edge-count g))
(print "expected p*C(12,2): " (math/round (* 0.4 66)))
(print "deterministic: " (edge-count (random-graph 12 0.4 7)))
(print "isolated vertices: "
       (length (filter (fn [u] (zero? (degree g u))) (nodes g))))
#+END_SRC

#+RESULTS:
: nodes: 12
: edges: 26
: expected p*C(12,2): 26
: deterministic: 26
: isolated vertices: 0

Note: $c = 0.4·12 = 4.8$, so the limiting proportion of isolated vertices is $e^(-4.8) ≈ 0.008$ the zero observed in a 12-vertex sample is consistent with that, but the sample is too small to illustrate the Poisson limit.
